Reading freeways from
the cells they hide. 8th place.

Ten synthetic freeway corridors, 5-minute detector data, and four tasks scored as one number: rebuild hidden speed and flow, forecast queues 30 minutes ahead, respect traffic physics, and estimate origin-destination demand. This page is the full record of our solution: the method, all 91 of our board reads with their April scores, the 267 ideas in our registry, and a build you can rerun.

The result

The private leaderboard is the April 2031 split. Nobody saw it before the close. Our scored file is C79; we stopped development on 21 October (last upload 20 October), 17 days before the close.

Final private leaderboard, top 16 shown

Where the points are

S_total = .35 state + .15 onset + .15 ongoing + .15 physics + .20 ODME. Bar length = weight; filled part = our estimated level on that component (board-implied, at C72; the five levels reproduce the board within .0001).

Points lost against a perfect score: physics .037, onset .025, state .019, ongoing .018, ODME .001. Physics (about .754) is our weakest component and ODME (.9933, at the organiser's own solver) the strongest.

Task 1 · state

Fill hidden cells

20%, 30% or 50% of eligible speed and flow cells are blanked per day. Score mixes speed RMSE /25 and lane-flow RMSE /600.

Task 2 · queues

Forecast queues 5 to 30 min ahead

40 onset and 40 ongoing windows per month, scored by space-time IoU. Online: only data stamped at or before the origin T. One window = .00375 S_total.

Task 3 · physics

Obey the fundamental diagram

Scored on our own Task 1 rows: one third FD fit, two thirds LWR mass conservation.

Task 4 · ODME

Estimate OD demand

Origin-destination flows from released link counts and a prior. We ship the organiser's own NNLS (λ = .05).

Exit 5

Measuring before shipping

Five uploads a day and one queue window worth .00375 S_total make the board a noisy judge. We wrote a forecast and a band for 90 of our 91 submissions before making them, and built an offline instrument for each component. The record says which instruments to trust.

The rule we learned. State and physics pieces learned and measured on the scored month read at a median 1.0× their forecast. New label sources read above their forecast (median 2.2×, 3 of 6 in band), because no offline test could see their full value. Members trained on train months read at .2× or below zero, and late adaptation pieces at a median −4.2×. We moved our effort to state pieces after this record.

Board noise: a state piece moves the board by its true effect within about .00001; a queue piece moves it in steps of up to .00375 per window. Adopted reads of +.00002 to +.00003 (rows 20, 30, 32, 34) are at that noise level.

Exit 6

Where the error lives

We scored the shipped chain at held-out INEL cells and split the error above a measured noise floor. The answer is narrow: queue fronts.

Improvable state error by slice

Share of cells (light bar) vs share of improvable error (dark bar). The first three rows partition the cells; the rest overlap. Improvable shares are computed per slice above that slice's noise floor, so the partition rows sum to a little over 100%.

Each new label source paid once

Public board gain per step of the label ladder. A second dose of the same labels added almost nothing. Hollow marker: S7c-INEL held out (+.00002); its read was against a forecast parent.

The queue decode bottleneck

Official windows that change as an in-model delta is scaled up. At its real size (1×) it changes one March window.

Fronts

2.2% of cells hold 85% of the improvable March error (79% in April). No released input we tried predicted it across dates.

Physics

Our cells sit on the published fundamental diagram closer than the observations do: FD loss .009 vs .015 at 968,856 held-out cells. Part of this is because predictions are smoother than observations, so it is a plausibility check, not a gain.

Decode

The median official queue window has no cell within .034 of the decode threshold, so small probability gains rarely flip a cell.

Exit 8

What failed, and how hard we looked

A negative result is only useful with its power. The last column is the smallest effect each test could have seen.

Train months misled queue models

A CNN beat persistence by +.115 on January and February but by +.015 on March. Bigger ensembles and longer pretraining won offline and lost on the board.

Pseudo windows misled corrections

Output corrections fitted on pseudo windows read the wrong sign. Adaptation inside the model read right.

ODME is at its floor

The hidden reference is the projection plus about 1.5% random per-path scatter. Every move away from the organiser's NNLS lost.

What we would do differently

Spend the late weeks on physics, our weakest component, instead of ongoing pieces that did not carry. Read queue pieces only as bundles that move several windows. Keep development running to the close: the teams above us submitted until 6 and 7 November.

Reproduce it

The repository rebuilds the scored file C79 (and C80) from the organisers' release and our saved weights, CPU only. Each step checks its output against our stored development file of that step, by sha256 or by value.

Steps

  1. Get the code and an environmentLinux / WSL2 Python 3.12. The base build expects its venv at this path because the fitted files record it. 
    git clone https://github.com/AminMohamed-3/trafficflowbench-2026
cd trafficflowbench-2026
uv venv --python 3.12 /workspace/tfb/venv
uv pip install --python /workspace/tfb/venv/bin/python -r environment/requirements-cpu.txt
export PATH=/workspace/tfb/venv/bin:$PATH
  2. Fetch data and weights: the competition data with your own Kaggle login; our weights (4.1 GB in 3 parts, plus 0.7 GB of stored intermediate files) from the GitHub release, sha256 checked 
    bash pipeline/00_fetch_data.sh
bash pipeline/01_fetch_weights.sh --release
  3. Build the base file C47Linux / WSL2 about 3.5 h with our fitted S08 files, about 9 h with a full refit (7 to 11.5 h by flags) 
    bash pipeline/10_base_c47.sh --s08-fitted
cat outputs/logs/base_c47.result
  4. Chain the 17 adopted piecesWindows Git Bash 12 to 13 h. The piece scripts call cygpath and the Windows venv's python.exe. 
    bash pipeline/20_pieces.sh
cat outputs/logs/chain_19_35.tsv

What "reproduced" means here

Scored file: C79, sha256 104c2c6f… (public .90088, private .89874). The chain also writes C80, which adds the April rows of rows 34 and 35 and was never uploaded.

C47 from code: queue and ODME rows equal; state rows within 4.2e-13 km/h of the submitted file.

Pieces 19 to 35: each step is compared with our stored development file of that step: sha256 equal, or value-equal (queue and ODME exact, state within 1e-8).

Stored outputs instead of re-serving: rows 21 (ConvNeXt-Tiny retrain), 22 (cold-date members), 23 (per-date Tiny adaptation) and 26 (S2b imputer members), because the originals ran on a GPU or another machine and floats differ in the last bits; and row 33 (Q-ACCUM: per-date fits inside Kaggle kernels, no weights). Exit 10

How it was run

All state lived in files, every upload had a written forecast first, and every idea got a verdict with the power of its test.

State in files

An idea registry (267 rows, one verdict class each), a ledger, a register of adopted pieces with build status, a calibration table of forecasts and board reads, and a digest of every user ruling.

Roles

Ideation rounds, each from different information, wrote ideas only. A separate ranking step compared them with the registry. Each line of work had a hypothesis, budget and kill condition, with a read-only review between attempts.

Guards

A pre-upload check allowed a submission only with a manifest line and a written forecast. A line of work could not edit the instrument that judged it.

Numbers

What went wrong in the process

Wrong headroom estimates sent two lines of work to ODME for days, and early coordination between parallel lines was mostly overhead. One member's weights were lost with a destroyed rented machine, and a cache later work needed was deleted. Each became a rule: save weights and a recipe at adoption.

Exit 11

Rules, leakage checks and disclosures

Online rule. Task 2 forecasts with origin T use only data stamped at or before T on that date, earlier dates of the same split, and train. Two read-only audits checked every Task 2 piece.

No identifiers as features. No IDs, row order, window numbering, mask hashes or generator seeds. Replays with permuted rows and changed IDs left every queue output unchanged.

INEL cells. Released cells the metric never scores. Their overlap with scored targets is 0 of 981,093 (March) and 0 of 948,791 (April).

Selection-implied inputs (U1). The organisers keep an ongoing window only if persistence IoU is at most .9. Our persistence cap (.92), the type-A raise and the twin's flags use that fact. They are computed from data at or before T. 43 of 80 windows are type A; the twin gave +.0045 public and +.0095 private.

Organiser ruling of 15 October. Everything used for a window, including model and threshold selection, is stamped at or before its T; April windows may use released March data. Every April choice uses only train months and released March data.

Declared April methods. Some April rows use a method chosen by a March board read, or April's own released view for offline Task 1 stages.

Open audit gaps. The future-poison test was not run inside the final build, and "no April data in a March row" holds by structure (one split per load), not by a mechanical test.

External weights. One ConvNeXt-Tiny with ImageNet-1k weights (Apache-2.0). No external data in any submitted file.

Checks you can run

  • checks/open_audit/: logs every file the build opens; the build reads nothing outside the repository and the competition data.
